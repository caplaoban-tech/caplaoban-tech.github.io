Trimegah Bangun Persada (IDX: NCKL) — Harita Nickel

Investor brief · price Rp740 (2026-11-02) · figures from IDX filings unless stated
Thesis. Nickel is at the bottom of its cycle and much of the industry is at or below breakeven — but NCKL is still growing profit and paying cash. It is a low-cost miner and processor with its own ore, its heavy investment phase is largely behind it, and its joint ventures (HPL, ONC, KPS) have started paying dividends. At ~4x earnings, a 5.8% trailing dividend yield (≈7% on 2026E) the market prices it as if the cycle bottom were permanent.

Nickel price: near the cycle low

LME 2026 monthly average: $17.8k (Jan) → $18.8k (May) → $15.5k (Oct). Chinese smelters are cutting output; Indonesia signals a lower 2027 ore quota (ESDM, 29.10.2026).

NCKL share price since IPO (Apr 2023)

The share has fallen with the Indonesian market (IHSG −41% YTD 2026, MSCI reclassification consultation), not with earnings.

Profit attributable to shareholders, by quarter (Rp trn)

Every quarter of 2026 is above the same quarter of 2025, despite lower nickel prices: higher ore volumes and prices (HPM), RKEF and a ramp-up of the KPS ferronickel JV.

The investment cycle is ending; cash is turning free (Rp trn)

Free cash flow = operating cash flow − capex − equity injections into JVs + dividends received from JVs. 9M 2026: JV injections down to Rp0.25 trn; first JV dividend (HPL, Rp0.24 trn in Q3).

How the shareholder gets paid

  1. Dividends: 30% of annual profit paid in cash — Rp42.64/share for FY2025 (paid 31 Jul 2026). On 2026E profit (~Rp10.9 trn) the same payout is ~Rp52/share.
  2. Own operations: mining (gross margin 45% in Q3 2026) and ferronickel (RKEF) — cash comes straight to NCKL.
  3. Joint ventures (HPL, ONC, KPS): their profit is booked by NCKL (Rp4.45 trn in 9M 2026), but cash arrives only as dividends; HPL resumed paying in Q3 2026 after the plant build-out.
  4. Re-rating: at 4x earnings, a return to 8x on stable profit (it traded at 10.9x a year ago) would roughly double the share.

Why upside is possible

  • Bottom of the cycle: high-pressure-acid (HPAL) producers across the industry are around or below breakeven with expensive sulphur and low nickel — that is not sustainable; supply cuts are already announced.
  • Strongest balance in the sector: own ore, low cost, profit growing while peers (e.g. MBMA) raise equity to fund themselves.
  • Cash conversion: spending on plants and JV injections fell from Rp6.7 trn (2023) and Rp6.0 trn (2025) to Rp0.7 trn in 9M 2026; JVs have started paying out.
  • Valuation: ~4x earnings vs 10.9x a year ago at Rp1,330 — profit is up 35%, so most of the fall is a lower multiple (market and MSCI), though operating cash flow has also weakened (see risks).

What must hold (risks)

  • New JV capex: HPL and ONC plan further expansion (Lygend IPO prospectus): NCKL's pro-rata share of the full budget is ~Rp7.5 trn through Q1 2028; part is already paid, the remaining schedule is not disclosed — it could absorb future JV dividends.
  • Working capital: inventories rose four quarters in a row (Rp5.3 trn → 10.3 trn); operating cash flow 9M 2026 Rp3.9 trn vs Rp7.3 trn a year earlier (−47%); gross profit −8.6%. The July dividend was partly funded with a new Rp1.07 trn loan.
  • Nickel and sulphur: another leg down in LME or a sulphur spike would hit JV profits first.
  • Market: MSCI reclassification of Indonesia (decision by 30 Jun 2027) and foreign outflows.
